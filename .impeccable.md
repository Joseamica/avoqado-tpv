## Design Context

### Users

Avoqado TPV is used by restaurant and venue staff—including waiters, cashiers,
managers, and administrators—on dedicated Android payment terminals. Operators
work quickly, frequently with one hand, and need controls that remain easy to
read and tap on small square and portrait screens. The primary job is to move
from an operational decision to a confirmed charge with minimal hesitation and
without input errors.

### Brand Personality

Operational, trustworthy, and accessible. The interface should feel like
purpose-built payment equipment: fast and calm under pressure, never decorative
at the expense of clarity.

### Aesthetic Direction

Maintain Avoqado's dark, high-contrast Material 3 visual system, DM Sans
typography, semantic theme colors, and restrained rounded geometry. Favor a
compact professional POS aesthetic inspired by dedicated payment terminals.
Support light mode through the existing theme, but do not introduce
device-specific branding or layouts keyed to hardware model names.

### Design Principles

1. Design touch-first: prioritize large, separated targets and legible labels.
2. Respond to available dimensions and aspect ratio, not manufacturer or model.
3. Preserve hierarchy and essential actions when vertical space is constrained.
4. Keep payment interactions visually stable and predictable across devices.
5. Protect performance on 1 GB terminals; prefer lightweight Compose layouts.
